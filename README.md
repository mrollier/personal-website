# michielrollier.be

Personal site. Astro, plain CSS, GitHub Pages.

## Run

```sh
nvm use          # Node 22
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:4321
npm run check    # build + link check
```

## Presenting offline

The public PhD defence (2 October 2026) is `/demos/defence/`: every slide as a live card in tabs, and a Present button. The deck lives in `src/components/defence/` (one component per block, `Deck.astro` for the shell) and `src/scripts/defence/` (`deck.ts` is the engine); its precomputed data in `src/data/defence/` comes from `npm run precompute` (`scripts/defence/precompute.ts`, seeded). The colours and the title mosaic follow the thesis cover (denim on apricot); the mosaic, `src/data/defence/mosaic.json`, comes from `python scripts/defence/export-mosaic.py`, which packs it with the cover code of `../game-of-life-mosaics` (it needs that repository, its level-7 tile census and a Python with numpy and scipy); its coast keeps clear of the text and logos of the title and closing slides, whose boxes are measured in the browser into `scripts/defence/title-boxes.json` (redo that when their text moves). The still-life slide packs a new multi-level landscape live on each reset (`pack` in `src/scripts/defence/cover.ts`, on the cover's pond lattice; `spark` there picks the cell to take away, one whose loss really spreads), and drains its colours away level by level before it is disturbed, as the closing slide does (`drain` in `mosaic.ts`); a few faint tiles peek in from the edge of most slides (`src/scripts/defence/sprig.ts`). From the thesis map on, a ribbon along the bottom (`Ribbon.astro`, chapters in `src/scripts/defence/ribbon.ts`) keeps the four chapters of the talk in view, two per half of the research question; the map slide flies them down into it, and every tile is a link to the first slide of its chapter. A Life-like network automaton's rule is drawn as two rings of intervals round a node, born and survive, which a click toggles (`src/scripts/defence/donut.ts`; its geometry in `rings.ts`, with which end of each interval it includes, at even resolutions too, and the rule's cousin); small networks with their nodes on and off are drawn by `netdraw.ts`, and from slide 12 on the talk uses the same three networks (`trio.ts`: random, small-world ring, scale-free). The synchronisation chapter is told as a mission, every node the same colour: a funnel of numbers down to 27 candidates, each step with a picture beside it (two rings, a rule that is its own cousin, the 27 curves), which then race on one grid from one start; `agree.ts` runs and judges a run, and `src/data/defence/consensus.json` holds the 27 candidates, the demo (whose grid and start the race uses too) and the trials. The classification slide computes each network's fingerprint live (`print.ts`: Shannon entropy, Lempel–Ziv complexity and neighbourhood density over the last 40 rounds), and then draws a fingerprint in their place, one textbook pattern per network (`fingerprint.ts`: a whorl, a loop, a tented arch, and a plain arch for all three once they look alike). The wildfire on the beyond slide is `wildfire.ts`: trees that burn for several rounds and catch from their eight neighbours, most easily downwind. Formulas, such as a rule's name, are LaTeX, typeset by KaTeX at build time (`src/components/defence/tex.ts`; a changing rule name swaps its numbers in place). The two metrics slides (the average next density and defect, with a draggable cobweb, a red tangent and three rules) draw with `src/scripts/defence/cobweb.ts`; the maths is in `curves.ts`, and `npm test` checks the numbers their speaker text quotes. The title slide's logos are in `public/demos/defence/logos/`: UGent and the Faculty of Bioscience Engineering (their official single-colour logos, recoloured from the white versions), USP and BionamiX. `npm run build` also writes `dist/demos/defence/rollier-defence.html` (`scripts/inline-defence.mjs`): the whole deck in one file, no network requests, which is the file to present from (with KaTeX's fonts as woff2 only). Two silent clips in `public/demos/defence/media/` are embedded in it: starlings around a peregrine falcon (C. Hemelrijk et al. 2019, figshare, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.8104724, videos 2 and 13 joined by a dissolve, with the end dissolved into the start) and an octopus in active sleep (A. Pophale et al., Nature 2023, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06203-4, Supplementary Video 1), both CC BY 4.0, trimmed (the octopus also cropped, without its sound, and its last second dissolved into its first so that it loops). What the speaker says with each slide is its `say` slot, shown under the card on the page and never in the deck.

- Launch: `firefox --kiosk "file:///path/to/rollier-defence.html"` gives true fullscreen without the fullscreen API, so Escape cannot end it. Press the clicker once to start. Without kiosk mode, click Start for fullscreen; Escape then leaves fullscreen but not the deck, and `f` goes back.
- Keys: PageDown, right arrow, space or `n` for the next build or slide; PageUp, left arrow, Backspace or `p` for back; `b` or `.` blanks the screen (the next press only unblanks); `f` fullscreen; `t` a rehearsal clock with the time the current block should end; digits and Enter jump to a slide; `Home` the start; `a` the backup slides; `c` on the rounds slide turns the countdown off; `r` on "One recipe, three outcomes" draws a new random start; `q` leaves (on the site). On a touchscreen a tap on the right half is next and on the left half back, except on a control, an interval of the rule rings, or a picture that is dragged (the flock, the cover landscape, the two curves); a mouse click never turns a slide. The pointer hides after a few still seconds. F5 and Ctrl+R do nothing while presenting.
- Test on the actual projector the day before: resolution, colours, and the clicker's buttons.
